The 10-minute rule

Only handle the next ten minutes.

When everything feels too big, shrink the job. Do the safest next ten minutes: slow your body, reduce risk, tell one person, and stay connected until the wave lowers.

Start the 10 minutes Find words

The next 10 minutes

This is not a test and not a promise about the whole day. It is just a short bridge from this moment to the next safer one.

0-2

Pause the motion

Sit down if you can. Put both feet on the floor. Breathe out slowly three times.

2-4

Make the room safer

Move closer to another person or a more open space. Put distance between you and anything risky.

4-7

Tell one person

Send one borrowed sentence. You do not have to explain the whole story to deserve help.

7-10

Stay with support

Call, text, sit near someone, or open a crisis chat. When ten minutes pass, choose the next ten.

If waiting ten minutes feels unsafe, skip the rule and get live help now. In the U.S., call or text 988, use 988 chat, or call emergency services.

Ground first

1Put both feet on the floor or press one hand against a wall.
2Breathe out slowly, like you are fogging a window.
3Name one thing that is true right now: "I am in this room. I am not alone forever."
4Choose the next support action, not the whole future.

Make the space safer

  • Move closer to other people if being alone feels risky.
  • Put distance between you and anything that makes harm easier.
  • Lower stimulation: dim lights, sit down, drink water, reduce noise.
  • Use a live support option if the danger feels immediate.

Words you can borrow

These are plain on purpose. You can send one exactly as written.

To a friend

"Can you stay with me?"

I am having a hard time and I do not want to be alone right now. Can you call me or sit with me?

To an adult

"I need help staying safe."

I am worried about myself and need support now. Can you help me contact someone?

To support

"I do not know how to explain it."

I am in crisis and need help. I may need you to ask simple questions.

After the wave passes

The next day does not have to be perfect. A simple support plan can make the next hard moment less lonely.

1

Choose two contacts

Pick one person for immediate support and one person for practical help, like scheduling care.

2

Write a short signal

Use a phrase like "I need the plan" so you do not have to explain everything during a crisis.

3

Set one check-in

Ask for a time to talk with a counselor, therapist, doctor, guardian, or trusted adult.
